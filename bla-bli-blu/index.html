My take

Bla Bli Blu has already solved cheap trial. The next win is not another offer; it is a reason to reorder a full bottle, and a way to reach buyers who came through a marketplace.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  2. Marketplace-heavy sales (reported) mean limited first-party contact. Capture it on the pack.
  3. Revenue and mix figures are reports or company claims. None drive the plan.

Section 4 of 19

Positioning

Gourmand, fruity and playful. Priced like a deal, framed like a treat.

  • Names read as personality: Mango So Goated, Berry Baddie, Watermelon Buddy, Love Drunk, Hot AF, My Wingman.
  • Ad copy leans on "25% oil concentration" and long-lasting wear (claims by the brand, not tested by me).
  • Tone is Gen Z humour: breakup lines, compliment promises, "vatavaran" creator content.
  • Gifting is positioned as a safe pick: sets sold as "never misses".
LaneExamplesWhere Bla Bli Blu sits
Mass body spray and deoEveryday, low priceBelow it on quality claim
Accessible premium D2CFraganote, Bella Vita, EM5Direct rival, cheaper entry
Luxury and nicheImported housesNot competing
My take

The brand voice is clear and fun. What is less visible is a promise about the scent itself: why this fragrance, for whom, on what occasion.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  1. Playful names win attention but give little help choosing.
  2. A "find your scent" journey would reduce choice anxiety.

Section 9 of 19

Creators & social

Creator content and gifting hooks are in the ad mix. Evidence on organic reach is thin.

  • One creator lip-sync ad ("vatavaran") ran 19 days. Style: trend audio, short, Gen Z.
  • Instagram is at 163.3K followers (23 Nov). I did not review post engagement.
  • Meltwater reports fragrance creator audiences are mostly women aged 18 to 34.
My take

Creators are used as ad creative more than as scent reviewers. A review-led series would answer the choice problem directly.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  1. Brief micro-creators to compare three scents on camera.
  2. Use creator whitelisting for the education angle.

11
Section 11 of 19

Acquisition

Offer-led paid social, with marketplaces and quick commerce doing the rest.

  • Paid social carries a ₹650 bundle and gift sets.
  • Search, GEO and organic search were not reviewed in depth (see section 17).
  • Newest tests (3 days old): Berry Baddie scent launch and ONE DRIVE car fragrance.
My take

A low-price entry brings volume, but it also brings low-intent buyers. I would split first-order cohorts by offer.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  1. Compare 90-day repeat for ₹650-bundle buyers and gift-set buyers.
  2. Retire creative in which the offer and scent do not match.

Section 12 of 19

Retention & CRM

Not visible from outside. A short list of what to check.

  • No public data on repeat rate, reorder timing or CRM. I did not sign up to test flows.
  • A 30 ml set lasts weeks; a reminder at that point is the obvious trigger.
  • My own lifecycle work: D30 retention moved 1.3% to 4.1% in a prior role (see my portfolio ledger).
My take

I would start with an audit of what customers receive after order one.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  1. Collect WhatsApp opt-in on pack and thank-you page.
  2. Time a reorder nudge to bottle size and scent family.

Section 14 of 19

Core growth plan

Turn a ₹650 trial into a repeat customer.

1 · Scent-finder quizOn pack QR and site. Result: a pick and a bundle. Opt-in to WhatsApp.
2 · Reorder journeyReminder timed to size. Offer a full-size credit.
3 · Review loopAsk on day 10. Feed best reviews to product pages and ads.
My take

Each step has a cheap first test and a clear metric.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  1. Ship the quiz first: it feeds every other step.
  2. Hold back discounts until the quiz is live, so I can tell what works.

Section 16 of 19

Trial, gifting & bundles

Already built. Refine, do not rebuild.

  • Existing: "pick any 3" bundle, 3 × 30 ml gift set, Gourmand Peti, a trial pack framed as "don't buy a full bottle and hope".
  • Test: a gift-note and QR so the receiver can reorder.
  • Test: festive vs everyday bundle at the same price.
  • Test: bundle cap so buyers upgrade to a full bottle with credit.
My take

Gifting has the longest-running ads. It is the strongest base to build repeat on.

Key Findings & Opportunities

  1. The gift receiver is a second customer. Today the brand has no visible way to reach them.
