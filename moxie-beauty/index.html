A growth plan for Moxie Beauty

Moxie grew fast on marketplaces and quick commerce. My question: how many of those first orders turn into a customer the brand can reach again, and what would I do to make a first order a hair routine?

Email ShubhamLinkedIn

By Shubham Mishra, growth and lifecycle marketer. Built from public information only.

The starting point

  • ₹100 Cr+annual run-rate revenue within two years of launch, as reported by the companyBeautyMatter
  • 4×growth in monthly revenue over the past year, as reported by the companyBeautyMatter
  • 40%+overall retention, as reported by the company. No time window is stated.Media Infoline
  • $15MSeries A in December 2025, led by Bessemer Venture PartnersBusiness Standard

Moxie sells on its own site, Nykaa, Amazon, Swiggy Instamart, Blinkit and Zepto. Inc42 lists ₹18.6 Cr+ of revenue for FY25, so the run-rate figure above reflects very recent growth. Inc42

What I read in the public numbers

  • A range built for routines. Listings show leave-in conditioner and curl cream at ₹289, serum at ₹549 and a hair mask at ₹749 (Clinikally). Low-priced steps make it natural to move a buyer from one product to two or three.
  • Much of the buying happens on other people’s platforms. Marketplaces and quick-commerce apps generally keep the customer’s contact details. If a large share of Moxie’s orders come from them, repeat purchases can happen without the brand being able to message the buyer. I do not know Moxie’s channel mix, so this is the first thing I would check.
  • “40%+ retention” needs a definition. Is it repeat within 90 days, 180 days or a year? Is it by customer or by order? Does it differ by channel and first product? Until that is clear, it cannot guide decisions.

The plan: five plays

1

Own the first order

Put a QR card in every pack, leading to a two-minute “find your routine” quiz on hair texture (wavy, frizzy or curly), climate and goals. Finishing it enrols the customer, with consent, in WhatsApp and email, in return for a styling guide or a sample. This brings marketplace and quick-commerce buyers into the brand’s own relationship.

Metric: share of orders that enrol
2

A routine journey, not a discount blast

Each journey starts from the product the customer bought. A curl cream buyer learns how to apply it, then hears about the leave-in that completes the routine, then the mask for deeper care. Timings are set from cohort data, not guessed.

Metric: second-product rate within 60 days
3

Reorder reminders by pack size

A 50 ml leave-in and a 200 ml mask run out at different times. I would time the reminder to the pack and point it at the fastest option for that customer: the brand’s site, Nykaa or quick commerce. Lapsed customers get a routine reminder before any offer.

Metric: days between orders · repeat rate
4

Reviews and community by hair type

Ask reviewers to tag texture and city, such as “wavy, humid Mumbai”, so a shopper sees results from hair like theirs. Add a wash-day challenge with a leaderboard where customers share their routine. Moxie already puts stats and reviews in its Instagram ads (Influencer Marketing Hub), so the best content feeds both product pages and ads.

Metric: reviews with hair-type tags · UGC a month
5

Launch to the base first

Each new product opens with a waitlist and early access for routine members, then goes wide on marketplaces. The first week’s sales come from people who already trust the brand, and their reviews are ready when everyone else arrives.

Metric: share of week-one units from existing customers

What the journey could look like

  • Day 0Welcome and how to use it, with a short video for the product they bought.
  • Early daysWash-day tip and a prompt to share a first result.
  • Week 2–3The next routine step, picked from what they bought.
  • Near run-outReorder reminder timed to pack size.
  • LapsedRoutine check-in, then a limited offer as a last step.

Illustrative timing. I would set the real windows from repeat-purchase data by product.

The first 90 days

Days 1 to 30: audit

  • Define repeat. Measure it by channel and first product.
  • Map first-to-second product paths.
  • Check what share of orders have a reachable contact.

Days 31 to 60: ship

  • Launch the pack QR and the quiz.
  • Go live with three journeys: welcome, next step, reorder.
  • Keep a holdout group to measure real lift.

Days 61 to 90: scale

  • Double down on what the holdout shows works.
  • Run the first wash-day challenge.
  • Share a one-page scorecard with the team.

How I would measure it
